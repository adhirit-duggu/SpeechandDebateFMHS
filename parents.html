Why We Need Volunteer Judges

High school Speech & Debate tournaments rely on volunteer adult judges to function. Schools must provide a required ratio of judges based on the number of student entries. No prior debate experience is required! Training is provided before every tournament.

What to Expect as a Judge

📋 Your Responsibilities

  • Listen to student speeches or debates attentively.
  • Keep track of time (timers provided on your phone).
  • Submit scores and brief constructive feedback online via Tabroom.com.

☕ Perks & Accommodation

  • Complimentary hospitality lounge with full meals, snacks, and coffee throughout the day.
  • Wi-Fi and dedicated quiet space between assigned rounds.
  • Directly lower your student's team judge bond/fees!

Getting Started

1. Create a Tabroom Account

All tournaments use Tabroom.com to assign rounds and collect online ballots. Create a free account to link with FMHS.

2. Watch NSDA Training Videos

The National Speech & Debate Association provides short 5-10 minute videos explaining how to judge each event type.

3. Attend On-Site Briefing

Every tournament hosts a morning orientation session for judges to ask questions before the first round starts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to have debate experience to judge?

Not at all! Tournaments encourage "lay judges" (everyday community members and parents) because students should learn to communicate persuasively to general audiences, not just debate experts.

How long is a tournament commitment?

Tournaments typically run on Saturdays. You can sign up for a full day (usually 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM) or a half-day shift (Morning or Afternoon) depending on your availability.

What do I need to bring with me?

A laptop or tablet with Wi-Fi capability is required to complete electronic ballots, along with a device charger and something comfortable to work on during downtime.
